## 📚 Training Curriculum

### Module 1: HIPAA Fundamentals (45 minutes)
**Audience:** All workforce members  
**Format:** Online course with quiz

#### 1.1 Introduction to HIPAA
**Learning Objectives:**
- Explain what HIPAA is and why it exists
- Identify the three main HIPAA rules
- Understand penalties for violations

**Content:**
- **What is HIPAA?**
  - Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (1996)
  - Federal law protecting patient health information
  - Applies to covered entities and business associates

- **Why HIPAA Matters:**
  - Protects patient privacy and trust
  - Prevents discrimination and identity theft
  - Ensures healthcare data security
  - RevClear is a **Business Associate** under HIPAA

- **The Three HIPAA Rules:**
  1. **Privacy Rule** - Who can see PHI and when
  2. **Security Rule** - How to protect electronic PHI (ePHI)
  3. **Breach Notification Rule** - What to do if PHI is exposed

- **HIPAA Penalties:**
  - **Civil:** $100 - $50,000 per violation (up to $1.5M per year)
  - **Criminal:** Up to 10 years in prison for intentional misuse
  - **Personal Liability:** Individuals can be held responsible
  - **Company Impact:** Loss of contracts, reputation damage

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What does HIPAA stand for?
2. What are the three main HIPAA rules?
3. Is RevClear a covered entity or business associate?
4. What is the maximum penalty for willful HIPAA violations?

---

#### 1.2 What is Protected Health Information (PHI)?
**Learning Objectives:**
- Define PHI and ePHI
- Identify 18 HIPAA identifiers
- Distinguish PHI from de-identified data

**Content:**
- **PHI Definition:**
  - Any health information that can identify an individual
  - Created, received, maintained, or transmitted by RevClear
  - Includes past, present, or future health information

- **18 HIPAA Identifiers:**
  1. Names (patient, relatives, employers)
  2. Geographic subdivisions smaller than state (zip code)
  3. Dates (birth, admission, discharge, death, dates >89 years old)
  4. Telephone numbers
  5. Fax numbers
  6. Email addresses
  7. Social Security numbers
  8. Medical record numbers
  9. Health plan beneficiary numbers
  10. Account numbers
  11. Certificate/license numbers
  12. Vehicle identifiers and serial numbers
  13. Device identifiers and serial numbers
  14. Web URLs
  15. IP addresses
  16. Biometric identifiers (fingerprints, voiceprints)
  17. Full-face photos
  18. Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code

- **ePHI (Electronic PHI):**
  - PHI stored or transmitted electronically
  - Databases, emails, cloud storage, backups
  - Subject to Security Rule requirements

- **Not PHI:**
  - De-identified data (all 18 identifiers removed)
  - Aggregate statistics (e.g., "50 patients this month")
  - Public health data without identifiers
  - Education records covered by FERPA

**Real-World Examples:**
- ✅ **IS PHI:** Patient name + diagnosis, medical record number, audio recording of patient visit
- ❌ **NOT PHI:** "Average claim value is $250", aggregate denial rates, CPT code list without patient info

**Knowledge Check:**
1. How many HIPAA identifiers are there?
2. Is a patient's zip code considered PHI?
3. What is the difference between PHI and ePHI?
4. Give three examples of PHI in RevClear's system.

#### 1.3 Uses and Disclosures of PHI
**Learning Objectives:**
- Explain when PHI use is permitted
- Apply "Minimum Necessary" principle
- Identify authorized vs. unauthorized disclosures

**Content:**
- **Permitted Uses (No Authorization Required):**
  1. **Treatment** - Care coordination, consultations
  2. **Payment** - Billing, claims, reimbursement (RevClear's primary function)
  3. **Operations** - Quality improvement, training, auditing
  4. **Required by Law** - Court orders, public health reporting
  5. **Patient Request** - Individual's right to access their own PHI

- **Minimum Necessary Standard:**
  - Only access PHI needed for your job
  - Don't look at records out of curiosity
  - Limit information in communications
  - **Example:** If coding a claim, view only that patient's encounter, not their entire history

- **NEVER Disclose PHI To:**
  - ❌ Family or friends (even if they ask nicely)
  - ❌ Other patients
  - ❌ Media or press
  - ❌ Social media
  - ❌ Personal email accounts
  - ❌ Unauthorized third parties
  - ❌ Colleagues without business need

- **Authorization Required For:**
  - Marketing purposes
  - Sale of PHI
  - Psychotherapy notes
  - Research (unless waived by IRB)
  - Uses beyond treatment/payment/operations

**Real-World Scenarios:**
- ✅ **OK:** Accessing patient record to code their claim
- ❌ **NOT OK:** Looking up celebrity patient out of curiosity
- ✅ **OK:** Discussing patient case with medical coder for billing
- ❌ **NOT OK:** Discussing patient case at lunch in public restaurant
- ✅ **OK:** Sending encrypted email with PHI to authorized clearinghouse
- ❌ **NOT OK:** Texting patient SSN to coworker

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What is the "Minimum Necessary" rule?
2. Can you access your coworker's medical record if they work at RevClear?
3. Name three situations where PHI disclosure requires patient authorization.
4. Is it OK to discuss patient information in a public elevator?

### Module 2: Security Safeguards (60 minutes)
**Audience:** All workforce members  
**Format:** Online course with interactive scenarios

#### 2.1 Physical Security
**Learning Objectives:**
- Practice clean desk policy
- Secure devices and media
- Protect PHI in physical spaces

**Content:**
- **Clean Desk Policy:**
  - ✅ Lock computer when leaving desk (Windows+L or Ctrl+Cmd+Q)
  - ✅ No PHI left on desk overnight
  - ✅ Shred paper documents with PHI
  - ✅ Store files in locked cabinets
  - ❌ Don't leave files visible to visitors

- **Device Security:**
  - ✅ Enable full-disk encryption (BitLocker/FileVault)
  - ✅ Use cable locks in public spaces
  - ✅ Never leave laptop unattended
  - ✅ Report lost/stolen devices immediately
  - ❌ Don't access PHI on public computers

- **Workspace Security:**
  - ✅ Position monitors away from windows/public view
  - ✅ Use privacy screens on laptops
  - ✅ Close sensitive documents when visitors arrive
  - ✅ Lower voice when discussing PHI
  - ❌ Don't hold PHI conversations in elevators, cafeteria, or public areas

- **Remote Work Security:**
  - ✅ Dedicated workspace (not coffee shop for PHI work)
  - ✅ Secure Wi-Fi with WPA3 encryption
  - ✅ Lock screens when leaving workstation
  - ✅ VPN required for system access
  - ❌ No family members using work computer

**Interactive Scenario:**
You're working from a coffee shop and need to review a patient claim. What should you do?
- A) Use the coffee shop Wi-Fi and work on the claim ❌
- B) Connect to VPN, use privacy screen, and be cautious ⚠️ (Acceptable but not ideal)
- C) Wait until you're home or in office to access PHI ✅ (Best practice)

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What should you do when leaving your desk for 10 minutes?
2. Is it OK to work on PHI at a coffee shop?
3. What should you do if your laptop is stolen?
4. Why should you use a privacy screen?

#### 2.2 Password and Authentication Security
**Learning Objectives:**
- Create strong, secure passwords
- Use multi-factor authentication properly
- Recognize password-related threats

**Content:**
- **Password Requirements:**
  - ✅ Minimum 14 characters
  - ✅ Mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols
  - ✅ Unique password for each system
  - ✅ Use password manager (1Password/Bitwarden)
  - ❌ Don't reuse passwords across systems
  - ❌ Don't share passwords with anyone
  - ❌ Don't write passwords on sticky notes

- **Good vs. Bad Passwords:**
  - ❌ **Weak:** password123, RevClear2025, John1234
  - ✅ **Strong:** Tr0pic@l-Mango#47!Sunset, C0rr3ct-H0rs3-B@ttery-St@ple

- **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):**
  - **What it is:** Second verification after password (something you have)
  - **Types:** Authenticator app (best), SMS code, hardware token
  - ✅ Always enable MFA when available
  - ✅ Keep backup codes in safe place
  - ❌ Don't disable MFA for convenience

- **Password Don'ts:**
  - ❌ Never share login credentials
  - ❌ Don't email passwords
  - ❌ Don't let browser auto-fill on shared computers
  - ❌ Don't use same password as personal accounts
  - ❌ Don't tell anyone your password (even IT - they can reset it)

- **Recognizing Password Attacks:**
  - **Phishing:** Fake emails asking for password
  - **Social Engineering:** Phone calls pretending to be IT
  - **Shoulder Surfing:** Someone watching you type password
  - **Brute Force:** Automated password guessing (defeated by account lockout)

**Interactive Scenario:**
You receive an email from "IT@revclear.com" saying your password expired and to click a link to reset it. What do you do?
- A) Click the link and enter your password ❌ (Phishing attack!)
- B) Ignore it ⚠️ (Might be legitimate)
- C) Forward to security@revclear.com and verify directly with IT ✅ (Correct)

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What is multi-factor authentication?
2. Can you share your password with your manager?
3. How long should your password be?
4. What should you do if someone asks for your password?

#### 2.3 Email and Communication Security
**Learning Objectives:**
- Send PHI securely via email
- Recognize phishing attempts
- Use approved communication channels

**Content:**
- **Email Security Rules:**
  - ✅ Use work email only for work communications
  - ✅ Encrypt emails containing PHI (TLS enabled)
  - ✅ Verify recipient address before sending
  - ✅ Use BCC for group emails (protect email addresses)
  - ❌ Don't forward PHI to personal email
  - ❌ Don't use personal email for work
  - ❌ Don't send PHI to unauthorized recipients

- **Phishing Red Flags:**
  - 🚩 Urgent language ("Act now or account will be closed!")
  - 🚩 Suspicious sender address (microsoftt.com, revclear-support.net)
  - 🚩 Generic greetings ("Dear user" instead of your name)
  - 🚩 Spelling and grammar errors
  - 🚩 Unexpected attachments or links
  - 🚩 Requests for passwords, SSN, or payment information
  - 🚩 Threats or pressure tactics

- **What to Do with Suspicious Emails:**
  1. Don't click links or open attachments
  2. Don't reply or provide information
  3. Forward to security@revclear.com
  4. Delete the email
  5. Report via phishing button (if available)

- **Safe Communication Channels:**
  - ✅ **RevClear web application** - Encrypted, audited
  - ✅ **Encrypted email** - TLS/SSL enabled
  - ✅ **Secure messaging (Slack)** - For internal discussions (no detailed PHI)
  - ✅ **Phone** - For sensitive discussions (verify caller)
  - ❌ **SMS/Text** - Not secure for PHI
  - ❌ **Social media** - Never discuss patients
  - ❌ **Public forums** - Never post PHI

- **Special Considerations:**
  - **Faxing:** Still used in healthcare, but verify recipient number
  - **Voicemail:** Don't leave detailed PHI (e.g., diagnosis) in voicemail
  - **Postal Mail:** Use sealed envelopes, no PHI visible on outside

**Interactive Scenario:**
You need to email a patient's claim details to a medical coder. How do you send it?
- A) Attach unencrypted PDF to regular email ❌
- B) Share link to patient record in RevClear system ✅ (Best)
- C) Copy and paste PHI into email body ❌
- D) Text the information ❌

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What are three red flags of a phishing email?

### Module 3: Recognizing and Reporting Security Incidents (30 minutes)
**Audience:** All workforce members  
**Format:** Scenario-based online training

#### 3.1 What is a Security Incident?
**Learning Objectives:**
- Define security incident
- Identify common incident types
- Understand reporting obligations

**Content:**
- **Security Incident Definition:**
  - Any attempted or successful unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, or destruction of PHI
  - Interference with system operations
  - Violation of security policies

- **Common Incident Types:**
  1. **Unauthorized PHI Access**
     - Accessing records you don't need for work
     - Snooping on coworker, family, or celebrity records
     - Intentionally viewing without authorization

  2. **Lost or Stolen Devices**
     - Laptop, phone, USB drive, or paper files
     - Unattended devices that go missing
     - Theft from car, office, or public space

  3. **Phishing Attacks**
     - Clicking malicious link
     - Providing credentials to fake site
     - Opening infected attachment

  4. **Malware/Ransomware**
     - Computer infected with virus
     - Ransomware encrypting files
     - Unusual pop-ups or system behavior

  5. **Accidental Disclosure**
     - Emailing PHI to wrong recipient
     - Leaving patient file in public area
     - Discussing patient in public where overheard

  6. **Insider Threats**
     - Employee stealing PHI
     - Unauthorized copying of database
     - Sabotage or malicious activity

  7. **System Vulnerabilities**
     - Unpatched software with known exploits
     - Misconfigured security settings
     - Weak passwords on shared accounts

- **"When in Doubt, Report":**
  - It's better to report a false alarm than miss a real incident
  - No retaliation for good-faith reporting
  - Early detection prevents larger breaches

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What is a security incident?
2. Give three examples of security incidents.
3. What should you do if you're unsure whether something is an incident?

---

#### 3.2 How to Report Security Incidents
**Learning Objectives:**
- Report incidents through proper channels
- Provide essential incident information
- Understand reporting timelines

**Content:**
- **Reporting Channels:**
  1. **Email:** security@revclear.com (monitored 24/7)
  2. **PagerDuty:** For urgent incidents (provided to authorized personnel)
  3. **Manager:** Notify your direct supervisor
  4. **Security Hotline:** [Phone TBD]

- **What to Report:**
  - ✅ Date and time of incident
  - ✅ What happened (brief description)
  - ✅ What systems or data were affected
  - ✅ Who was involved (if known)
  - ✅ Your contact information
  - ✅ Any evidence (screenshots, emails)

- **Reporting Timeline:**
  - **Immediately:** Active threats (malware, unauthorized access, data breach)
  - **Same day:** Lost devices, accidental disclosures
  - **Within 24 hours:** Suspicious activity, policy violations

- **What NOT to Do:**
  - ❌ Don't try to fix it yourself (could destroy evidence)
  - ❌ Don't delete or modify files
  - ❌ Don't tell others before reporting (gossip spreads)
  - ❌ Don't post on social media
  - ❌ Don't ignore it hoping it goes away

- **What Happens After You Report:**
  1. Security team acknowledges receipt
  2. Incident is triaged and investigated
  3. You may be contacted for more information
  4. Incident is resolved and documented
  5. You receive follow-up (lessons learned)

- **No Retaliation Policy:**
  - Good-faith reporting is protected
  - Honest mistakes happen
  - We learn and improve from incidents
  - Intentional violations are different from accidents

**Real-World Examples:**
- ✅ **Report:** "I accidentally emailed a patient's claim to the wrong medical coder. I realized it within 5 minutes."
- ✅ **Report:** "I clicked a link in an email that looked suspicious. My computer is acting strange now."
- ✅ **Report:** "I saw a coworker accessing patient records they don't work with."
- ✅ **Report:** "I left my laptop in a conference room overnight. It's now missing."

**Interactive Scenario:**
You accidentally send an email containing patient information to the wrong recipient. What do you do?
1. Reply to the wrong recipient asking them to delete it
2. Report to security@revclear.com immediately ✅
3. Delete the sent email from your sent folder
4. Don't tell anyone and hope they don't open it

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What email address should you use to report security incidents?
2. What information should you include in an incident report?
3. What should you NOT do after discovering an incident?
4. Will you get in trouble for reporting an honest mistake?
